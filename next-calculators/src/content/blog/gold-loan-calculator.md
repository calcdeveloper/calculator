## Formula and Calculation Method

To understand why the lender's offer is lower than the live TV market ticker, you must understand the two-step formula the calculator executes in the background.

**Step 1: Calculating the Pure Value**
The calculator must adjust the daily rate (which is for 24k pure gold) to match the actual purity of your jewelry.
*Adjusted Rate = (Live 24k Gold Rate) × (Your Carat ÷ 24)*
*Total Market Value = Total Weight in Grams × Adjusted Rate*

**Step 2: Applying the Loan-to-Value (LTV) Ratio**
Banks need a safety margin. If gold prices crash tomorrow, they need to ensure the gold they are holding is still worth more than the money they lent you. 
*Maximum Loan Amount = Total Market Value × (LTV %)*

*Why this matters:* If you have 100 grams of 22k gold, and the 24k rate is $75/gram, the market value is not $7,500. It is (100g × $75 × (22/24)) = $6,875. If the LTV is 75%, your actual loan is only $5,156. The calculator bridges the gap between the $7,500 fantasy and the $5,156 reality.

## Common Mistakes Users Make

Gold valuation is a highly regulated, mathematically strict process. Avoid these frequent calculation pitfalls that lead to severe disappointment at the lender's desk:

1.  **Ignoring Carat Purity:** This is the most common error. If you weigh 100 grams of 14-carat gold and multiply it by the 24-carat daily rate, your valuation will be wildly inflated. 14-carat gold is only 58.3% gold! Always ensure the calculator's purity dropdown exactly matches the hallmark on your jewelry.
2.  **Assuming 100% LTV:** Regulators (like the RBI in India) strictly forbid banks from lending 100% of the gold's value. This is to protect the banking system from a sudden crash in global gold prices. The maximum is usually capped at 75%. Never calculate your emergency cash needs based on a 100% valuation.
3.  **Forgetting "Making Charges":** When you bought the gold jewelry, you paid a 15% to 20% premium for "Making Charges" (the craftsmanship). The bank does not care about craftsmanship. They value the asset strictly on its melted bullion weight. You must mentally accept that the loan value will be significantly lower than the original retail purchase price of the jewelry.
4.  **Misunderstanding the Bullet Repayment:** Many gold loans allow you to pay only the interest every month. However, at the end of the 1-year tenure, the entire massive principal amount is due on a single day. If you fail to make that bullet payment, the bank has the legal right to instantly auction your gold.

## Frequently Asked Questions (FAQs)

**1. What does LTV mean in a Gold Loan?**
LTV stands for Loan-to-Value. It is the percentage of the gold's total market value that the bank is willing to lend you. If your gold is worth $10,000 and the bank's LTV is 75%, they will give you a maximum loan of $7,500.

**2. Why did the bank value my gold lower than the calculator?**
There are three main reasons: 1) Your gold has a lower purity than you thought. 2) The bank deducted the weight of non-gold elements (stones, enamel, clasps). 3) The bank used an average gold price over the last 30 days, rather than today's specific live peak price.

**3. Does my CIBIL/Credit Score matter for a Gold Loan?**
Generally, no. Because the loan is fully secured by physical gold (which the bank holds in a vault), your credit history is mostly irrelevant. This makes gold loans the best option for people with poor credit scores.

**4. What happens if I don't repay the Gold Loan?**
The bank will issue a series of legal notices. If you still fail to pay, the bank will publicly auction your gold to recover their principal and outstanding interest. Any excess money generated from the auction (after fees) is legally required to be returned to you.

**5. How is the interest calculated on a Gold Loan?**
Most gold loans calculate interest on a daily reducing balance. However, repayment structures vary. You can choose a standard EMI, or you can choose to pay only the interest every month and pay the principal at the very end of the tenure.

**6. Can I get a loan against 24-carat gold bars?**
Regulations vary by country. In India, for example, the RBI prohibits banks from granting loans against pure 24-carat gold bullion bars or coins weighing more than 50 grams. Loans are primarily encouraged against gold jewelry.

**7. Does the value of my loan change if the price of gold crashes?**
Once the loan is disbursed, your principal owed does not change. However, if gold prices crash so severely that the market value of your pledged gold falls below your outstanding loan balance, the bank will issue a "Margin Call," forcing you to deposit cash immediately to bridge the gap.
