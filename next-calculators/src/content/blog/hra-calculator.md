## Formula and Calculation Method

The calculator utilizes the exact comparative algorithm mandated by Section 10(13A) of the Income Tax Act.

The Tax-Exempt HRA is the **LOWEST** of the following three amounts:

**Condition 1:** The Actual HRA received from your employer.
**Condition 2:** 50% of your Basic Salary (if living in a Metro city) OR 40% of your Basic Salary (if living in a Non-Metro city).
**Condition 3:** Actual Rent Paid minus 10% of your Basic Salary.

*Calculation Logic:* The tool calculates the value for all three conditions. Whichever number is the smallest becomes your official tax-exempt HRA. Any HRA you received from your employer *above* this exempted amount is added back to your taxable income.

## Common Mistakes Users Make

When claiming HRA, taxpayers frequently fall into traps that result in disallowed exemptions and stressful audits. Avoid these frequent pitfalls:

1.  **Assuming 100% of HRA is Tax-Free:** This is the most dangerous assumption. As the calculator shows, if you pay very little rent relative to your high Basic Salary, a massive portion of your HRA will become taxable.
2.  **Confusing Metro and Non-Metro Cities:** The Income Tax Act is very strict. Only Mumbai, Delhi, Kolkata, and Chennai are considered Metro cities for HRA purposes. Major IT hubs like Bengaluru, Hyderabad, and Pune are strictly classified as Non-Metro (40% limit). Inputting the wrong city type will result in a rejected tax filing.
3.  **Claiming HRA While Paying a Mortgage:** You cannot claim HRA for a house you own. HRA is strictly an allowance for rented accommodation. (However, if you own a house in one city but pay rent in another city due to employment, you can claim both HRA and home loan interest deductions simultaneously).
4.  **Forgetting the PAN Card Rule:** If your annual rent paid exceeds ₹1,00,000 (approx. ₹8,333 per month), you are legally required to provide your landlord's PAN (Permanent Account Number) to your employer to claim the exemption. If the landlord refuses to give it, your HRA claim will be rejected.

## Frequently Asked Questions (FAQs)

**1. Is HRA exemption available in the New Tax Regime?**
No. The New Tax Regime explicitly abolishes the House Rent Allowance exemption. To claim HRA, you must opt for the Old Tax Regime when filing your taxes.

**2. Can I claim HRA if I live with my parents?**
Yes, absolutely. You can legally claim HRA by paying rent to your parents. However, you must have a formal rent agreement with them, transfer the money via bank transfer (not cash), and your parents must declare this rent as income on their own tax returns.

**3. Can I claim HRA if I pay rent to my spouse?**
No. The Income Tax Department heavily scrutinizes rent paid to a spouse and generally disallows it, viewing it as a sham transaction to evade taxes since husband and wife are considered a single economic unit for housing purposes.

**4. What if my landlord refuses to give me their PAN card?**
If your total annual rent is above ₹1,00,000, providing the landlord's PAN is mandatory. If they refuse, you cannot claim the HRA exemption through your employer. (Some landlords refuse because they are illegally hiding rental income from the government).

**5. I own a house and pay an EMI. Can I still claim HRA?**
Yes, but only under specific circumstances. If you own a house in City A (and claim tax benefits on the home loan EMI), but your job requires you to live and pay rent in City B, you can legally claim both the Home Loan deduction and the HRA exemption simultaneously.

**6. Why is Bengaluru considered a Non-Metro for HRA?**
Despite being a massive, expensive IT hub, the Income Tax Act definition of "Metro" for HRA purposes has not been updated in decades. It still strictly only includes the four historical metro cities: Delhi, Mumbai, Chennai, and Kolkata. All other cities, including Bengaluru, Hyderabad, and Pune, are restricted to the 40% Non-Metro rule.

**7. Do I need to submit rent receipts every month?**
Most employers require you to submit rent receipts and a copy of your lease agreement once a year (usually in January or February) as proof before they finalize your Form 16 and allow the HRA exemption.
