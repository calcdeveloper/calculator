To make your life easier, high-quality generators utilize your browser's `localStorage` API. If you close your browser tab by accident while filling out an invoice, the tool saves your data locally. When you reopen the page, your company logo, address, and client details are instantly restored, preventing you from ever losing your hard work.

## Common Mistakes Users Make

Even though the tool provides absolute formatting perfection, the human element of *deploying* the invoice can ruin a client relationship or delay your payment. Avoid these massive traps:

1.  **Forgetting to Set a Due Date (Payment Terms):** If you do not explicitly state when the invoice is due, the client will push it to the absolute bottom of their priority list. You might wait three months to get paid! Always set clear terms, such as "Net 30" (payment is due within 30 days of the issue date) or "Due Upon Receipt."
2.  **Using Vague Descriptions:** If a line item simply says "Work - 500," the client's accounting department will reject it because they don't know what they are paying for. Always be incredibly specific. Write "Search Engine Optimization for Homepage (April 1 - April 15) - 500." The more specific you are, the faster the accountant will approve it.
3.  **Sending Late Invoices:** If you finish a project and wait three weeks to send the invoice, the client loses the psychological momentum of receiving the delivered work. They might even forget what they agreed to pay. You should generate and email your invoice within 24 hours of completing a project to ensure rapid payment.

## Frequently Asked Questions (FAQs)

**1. What is an Invoice Number and how do I format it?**
An invoice number is a unique sequential code used by accounting departments to track payments. You can format it however you like! Many freelancers start with `001`. A highly professional trick is to use the year and the client's initials (e.g., `2026-ABC-001`). Just ensure you never use the same number twice!

**2. Is this invoice a legally binding document?**
An invoice is a commercial instrument that acts as a formal request for payment. While it is a critical piece of accounting documentation, it is the *contract* you signed with the client before the work started that makes the payment legally binding.

**7. Should I send the invoice as a link or an attachment?**
You should always download the PDF from our tool and send it as an email attachment. Sending a link to a web page is highly unprofessional, as web links can break, expire, or get blocked by corporate firewalls. A downloaded PDF is a permanent, secure file.

**8. Is it okay to charge late fees?**
Yes, but you must establish this rule *before* you send the invoice. If your initial contract states that a 5% late fee will be applied after 30 days, you can absolutely add a line item for a "Late Fee" on a subsequent invoice if the client fails to pay on time.
