## Frequently Asked Questions (FAQs)

**1. What is a Turns Ratio?**
It is a mathematical representation of the physical difference between the two copper coils inside a transformer. It is calculated by dividing the number of wire wraps on the Input (Primary) by the number of wraps on the Output (Secondary).

**2. What is the difference between a Step-Up and a Step-Down transformer?**
A Step-Down transformer (e.g., 10:1 ratio) takes high, dangerous wall voltage and crushes it down to a low, safe voltage for electronics. A Step-Up transformer (e.g., 1:10 ratio) takes low voltage and violently multiplies it to power high-voltage equipment like neon signs or microwaves.

The copper wire itself is heavy, but the vast majority of the weight comes from the solid iron or steel core. The iron acts as a highway for the invisible magnetic field, allowing it to perfectly travel from the primary coil to the secondary coil.

Transformers require a constantly moving, pulsing magnetic field to "induce" electricity in the secondary coil. AC power pulses (alternates) 50 to 60 times a second, creating that pulse. DC power is flat and constant; it creates a static magnetic field that does nothing.

**6. Does a transformer create free energy?**
No. This is the Law of Conservation of Energy. If a transformer increases the Voltage by 10, it MUST simultaneously decrease the Current (Amps) by 10. The total Power (Watts = Volts x Amps) always remains exactly the same on both sides (minus a tiny bit of heat loss).

It is a safety transformer with a strict 1:1 ratio. It does not change the voltage at all (120V in, 120V out). Its only purpose is to physically disconnect the device from the wall outlet grid.
