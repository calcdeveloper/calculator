## Formula and Calculation Method

The calculator uses the standard compound interest formula to determine the maturity amount.

**The Formula for Compound Interest FD is:**
A = P (1 + r/n)^(n*t)

Where:
*   `A` = Maturity Amount
*   `P` = Principal amount deposited
*   `r` = Annual interest rate (in decimal, so 7% becomes 0.07)
*   `n` = Number of times interest is compounded per year (e.g., 4 for quarterly)
*   `t` = Time period in years

*Note: For simple interest FDs (usually tenures less than 6 months), the formula is A = P + (P × r × t / 100).*

## Common Mistakes Users Make

When planning FD investments, try to avoid these pitfalls:

1.  **Ignoring Inflation:** FD returns are safe, but they often struggle to beat inflation. Don't put 100% of your wealth in FDs if your goal is long-term wealth creation.
2.  **Not Comparing Compounding Frequencies:** A 7% FD compounded quarterly yields more than a 7.1% FD compounded annually over the long term. Always check the compounding frequency.
3.  **Forgetting Taxes (TDS):** FD interest is fully taxable as per your income tax slab. Banks will deduct a 10% TDS if your interest exceeds ₹40,000 (₹50,000 for senior citizens) in a year.
4.  **Premature Withdrawals:** Breaking an FD early usually attracts a penalty (around 0.5% to 1%) and lowers your overall returns. Plan your liquidity needs carefully before locking in the tenure.

## Frequently Asked Questions (FAQs)

**1. What is the difference between simple interest and compound interest in an FD?**
Simple interest is calculated only on the principal amount. Compound interest is calculated on the principal *plus* the accumulated interest from previous periods, meaning your "interest earns interest," leading to higher overall returns.

**2. How often is interest compounded in Indian banks?**
Most major Indian banks compound FD interest on a quarterly basis (every 3 months).

**3. Are FD returns guaranteed?**
Yes, FD returns are guaranteed by the bank, unaffected by market fluctuations. Additionally, deposits up to ₹5 Lakhs per bank are insured by the DICGC (a subsidiary of RBI).

**4. How is FD interest taxed?**
The interest earned is added to your total income and taxed according to your applicable income tax slab. Banks deduct a 10% TDS if the interest exceeds the specified threshold.

**5. Can I get a monthly income from my FD?**
Yes, you can choose a non-cumulative payout option where the interest is credited to your savings account monthly, quarterly, half-yearly, or annually, rather than being reinvested.

**6. Does the FD Calculator account for TDS?**
A standard FD calculator shows the gross maturity amount. It does not automatically deduct TDS, as the tax liability varies depending on the individual's overall income tax bracket.

**7. Should senior citizens expect different results?**
Yes, banks in India generally offer an additional 0.50% to 0.75% interest rate to senior citizens (individuals aged 60 and above). You should input this higher rate into the calculator.
