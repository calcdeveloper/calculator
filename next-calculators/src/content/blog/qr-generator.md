## How does the Tool work? (The Technology)

It is easy to look at a QR code and assume it is just random static, but it is actually a highly sophisticated, mathematically brilliant piece of data engineering. How does a camera translate black and white squares into a website? 

Here is a look at the fascinating computer science and geometry used behind the scenes:

### 1. The Position Detection Patterns (The "Eyes")
If you look closely at any QR code, you will notice three large squares located in the top-left, top-right, and bottom-left corners. These are the "Eyes." They are arguably the most brilliant part of the design. They tell the smartphone camera exactly where the edges of the code are, what size it is, and what angle it is being held at. This is why you can scan a QR code upside down or at a strange 45-degree angle, and the camera will still read it perfectly!

### 2. Binary Encoding (Modules)
The tiny black and white squares inside the grid are called "Modules." Just like the hard drive on a computer, the code uses binary logic. A black square represents a "1" and a white square represents a "0". Our generator translates your text into this binary data stream and snakes it through the grid in a very specific zig-zag pattern, starting from the bottom right corner.

### 3. Reed-Solomon Error Correction
This is the true superpower of the QR code. The algorithm uses a highly advanced mathematical concept called "Reed-Solomon Error Correction." When our tool builds the code, it mathematically duplicates and scrambles the data throughout the grid. This means that if you print a QR code on a poster, and someone spills coffee on it, rips a corner off, or folds it in half, the code can *still* be scanned perfectly! Depending on the error correction level, up to 30% of the QR code can be completely destroyed, and the camera can still recover the lost data.

## Common Mistakes Users Make

Even though the tool provides absolute mathematical perfection, the human element of *deploying* the QR code can ruin the marketing campaign. Avoid these massive traps:

1.  **Printing Too Small:** If you generate a highly complex QR code (like a massive URL) and print it on a business card at the size of a postage stamp, a smartphone camera will not have the optical resolution to read the tiny squares. As a general rule, a QR code should never be printed smaller than 1 inch by 1 inch (2.5 cm).
2.  **Lack of Contrast:** A QR code relies on the camera's ability to distinguish the modules from the background. The most reliable format is always pure black squares on a pure white background. If you generate a light yellow QR code and print it on a white piece of paper, the camera will fail to read it. Always ensure massive visual contrast!
3.  **Missing a "Call to Action":** If you just print a naked, random black square on a poster, nobody is going to scan it. People are naturally suspicious. You must always include human text next to the code! (e.g., *"Scan Here for 10% Off!"* or *"Scan to view our Menu!"*). Tell the user exactly *why* they should pull their camera out.

The "Static" QR codes generated by our tool will **never expire**. The data (like your website URL) is hardcoded directly into the black and white squares. As long as your website exists, and the physical printout isn't destroyed, the code will work for 100 years.

**3. Do users need to download a special app to scan them?**
No! Ten years ago, you needed a third-party app. Today, Apple (iOS) and Android have built QR scanning technology directly into their native camera apps. Users simply open their standard camera, point it at the code, and a clickable link will instantly appear on their screen.

**4. Are my generated QR codes saved or tracked by the website?**
No, absolutely not! This is a massive privacy concern for users, and we take it incredibly seriously. The entire encoding generation process happens locally inside your web browser session. We do not store, track, transmit, or record the links or data you generate.

**5. What is the difference between a Static and Dynamic QR code?**
A Static QR code (which we generate) holds the raw data directly. You cannot change the URL after it is printed. A Dynamic QR code holds a "redirect" link to a server, allowing you to log in and change the final destination later. However, Dynamic codes are almost always hidden behind expensive monthly subscription paywalls by marketing companies. Our Static codes are 100% free.

**6. Can I use the generator on my mobile phone?**

**7. Why does my QR code look so dense and complicated?**
The more data you put into a QR code, the more "Modules" (tiny squares) it requires to hold that data. A short link like `google.com` will generate a very simple, blocky code. A massive, 200-character URL will generate a highly complex, dense code. For the easiest scanning, always try to use short URLs!

**8. Can I use these QR codes for my commercial business?**
