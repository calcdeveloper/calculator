## How does the PF Calculator work?

The mechanics of the calculator are rooted in the strict statutory rules of the Employees' Provident Fund Organization (EPFO). To generate an accurate forecast, it requires a few key inputs:

1.  **Current Age and Retirement Age:** This dictates the "time horizon" for the compounding interest. (Retirement age for PF maturity is typically 58).
2.  **Basic Salary (+ Dearness Allowance):** PF is strictly calculated as a percentage of your Basic Salary + DA, never your total Gross CTC.
3.  **Current PF Balance:** The amount of money currently sitting in your EPF account from past years of employment.
4.  **Expected Annual Salary Increase:** Because your basic salary usually increases every year due to appraisals, your PF contribution also increases annually. The tool factors in this step-up contribution.
5.  **Current Interest Rate:** The interest rate declared by the government for the current financial year (e.g., 8.15% to 8.25%).

Once you input these parameters, the computational engine simulates your career year-by-year. It calculates your 12% contribution, splits the employer's contribution between PF and EPS, adds the annual interest to the opening balance, steps up the salary for the next year, and repeats the cycle until your retirement age.

## Formula and Calculation Method

The calculator utilizes a complex yearly looping algorithm to account for the EPS diversion and the step-up in salary.

**The Statutory Breakdown (India EPF Rules):**
1.  **Employee Contribution:** 12% of Basic Salary goes entirely to the EPF account.
2.  **Employer Contribution:** 12% of Basic Salary is provided, but it is split:
    *   **EPS (Pension):** 8.33% of Basic Salary (Capped at a maximum of ₹1,250 per month, assuming a ₹15,000 salary cap).
    *   **EPF (Provident Fund):** The remaining balance (12% minus the EPS amount) goes into the EPF account. (Usually 3.67% for salaries below 15k, but functionally much higher for larger salaries due to the ₹1,250 cap).

**Interest Calculation:**
Interest is calculated monthly on the closing balance but is credited annually at the end of the financial year. The formula applies compound interest year over year, creating an exponential growth curve.

1.  **Withdrawing PF When Changing Jobs:** This is the most destructive financial mistake an employee can make. When you switch jobs, you can easily transfer your PF account. 2.  **Assuming the Employer's 12% is Fully Yours:** As the calculator shows, the employer's 12% is split. A portion is diverted to the government EPS (pension) scheme. This pension pool does not earn compound interest in the same way, and you cannot withdraw it as a lump sum easily.

High earners must factor this in when maxing out VPF.

## Frequently Asked Questions (FAQs)

**1. Is EPF mandatory for all employees?**
In India, EPF is mandatory for employees earning a basic salary up to ₹15,000 per month in organizations with 20 or more employees. However, almost all corporate employers extend it to all employees regardless of salary as a standard benefit.

**2. Can I withdraw my PF balance before retirement?**
Yes, but with strict conditions. You can make partial withdrawals (advances) for specific reasons like medical emergencies, purchasing a house, or a child's higher education/marriage. If you remain unemployed for more than 2 months, you can legally withdraw the entire balance.

**3. What happens to my PF if I quit my job and start a business?**
Your PF account continues to earn interest even if no new contributions are being made. However, if no contributions are made for 36 months (3 years), the account is classified as "inoperative," though recent rule changes state it may continue to earn interest until age 58.

**4. Is the interest earned on PF totally tax-free?**
Yes, under the EEE status, the interest is tax-free. However, as of recent budgets, if your *employee* contribution (EPF + VPF) exceeds ₹2.5 Lakhs in a single financial year, the interest earned on the contribution *above* ₹2.5 Lakhs will be taxed according to your income tax slab.

**5. What is the difference between EPF and PPF?**
EPF (Employees' Provident Fund) is linked to your salary, requires an employer match, and currently yields around 8.15%. PPF (Public Provident Fund) is a voluntary scheme open to anyone (including non-salaried individuals), has a 15-year lock-in, no employer match, and currently yields around 7.1%.

**6. Do I get compound interest on the employer's EPS contribution?**
No. The 8.33% diverted to the Employee Pension Scheme (EPS) goes into a pooled government fund. It does not earn compound interest for your personal account. Instead, it qualifies you to receive a fixed monthly pension from the government after you turn 58.

**7. Should I transfer or withdraw my PF when changing companies?**
You should **always transfer** it. Transferring it via the UAN (Universal Account Number) portal is now completely digital and seamless. Transferring keeps the magic of compound interest unbroken.
