## Common Mistakes Users Make

Taxation is complicated, and misunderstandings can lead to severe financial penalties or missed opportunities. Avoid these frequent pitfalls:

1.  **Confusing Old Regime with New Regime:** The biggest mistake is trying to claim deductions for House Rent Allowance (HRA), Section 80C (PPF, ELSS, Life Insurance), or Section 80D (Health Insurance) while using the New Tax Regime. The new regime explicitly disallows almost all of these deductions in exchange for lower baseline rates.
2.  **Forgetting the Standard Deduction:** When the new regime was first launched, there was no standard deduction. It was recently introduced. Many taxpayers still calculate their new regime taxes based on their Gross Salary, forgetting to manually subtract the ₹50,000 standard deduction, resulting in an artificially high tax estimate.
3.  **Misunderstanding the ₹7 Lakh Rebate:** If your income is ₹7,00,000, you pay zero tax. But if your income is ₹7,10,000, you don't just pay tax on the ₹10,000. You lose the entire rebate and must pay tax according to the normal slabs (which equates to over ₹26,000). The calculator visually highlights this massive "cliff edge."
4.  **Ignoring the Health & Education Cess:** Many people manually calculate their tax based on the slab percentages and stop there. They forget that the government mandates an additional 4% cess on top of the calculated tax amount. The calculator adds this automatically.

## Frequently Asked Questions (FAQs)

**1. What is the difference between the Old Tax Regime and the New Tax Regime?**
The Old Regime has higher tax rates but allows you to claim numerous exemptions (like HRA, LTA) and deductions (like Section 80C, 80D) to lower your taxable income. The New Regime has much lower tax rates but removes almost 70+ exemptions and deductions, offering a simpler, flat calculation.

**2. Is the New Tax Regime mandatory?**
In India, the New Tax Regime is now the *default* regime. However, it is not mandatory. Salaried taxpayers still have the option to choose the Old Regime when filing their returns if it is more beneficial to them (usually true if they have high investments in home loans, PPF, and health insurance).

**3. Does the New Regime allow the ₹50,000 Standard Deduction?**
Yes. In recent budgets, the government extended the ₹50,000 Standard Deduction to salaried employees and pensioners under the New Tax Regime as well. (Business income is not eligible for this specific deduction).

**4. How does the ₹7 Lakh Tax Rebate work?**
Under Section 87A, if your Net Taxable Income (after the standard deduction) is ₹7,00,000 or less, you get a 100% tax rebate. This means you legally owe ₹0 in taxes. However, if your income crosses ₹7 Lakhs even by ₹1, the rebate is completely cancelled.

**5. What is Marginal Relief?**
Because losing the ₹7 Lakh rebate over a tiny income increase (like earning ₹7,10,000) results in a tax bill (e.g., ₹26,000) that is larger than the extra income earned (₹10,000), the government offers "Marginal Relief." This ensures the tax payable cannot exceed the income earned above the ₹7 Lakh threshold. Our calculator automatically factors this complex relief into the final output.

**6. Do I have to pay tax if my income is below the basic exemption limit?**
The basic exemption limit under the New Regime is ₹3,00,000. If your income is below this, you owe no tax and theoretically do not need to file a return (though filing a Nil return is highly recommended for building financial history for loans and visas).

**7. Can I claim HRA (House Rent Allowance) under the New Regime?**
No. HRA exemptions are strictly abolished under the New Tax Regime. If you pay a high amount of rent, you should calculate your taxes under the Old Regime to see if it saves you more money.

**8. Does the calculator include the Surcharge for high earners?**
Yes, a comprehensive calculator will automatically apply the mandatory surcharges. For example, if your income exceeds ₹50 Lakhs, a 10% surcharge is added to your tax. If it exceeds ₹1 Crore, a 15% surcharge is added. The New Regime recently capped the highest surcharge at 25% (down from 37% in the old regime).
