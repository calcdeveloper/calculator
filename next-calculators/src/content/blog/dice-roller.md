## How does the Tool work? (The Algorithm)

It might seem like digital magic, but simulating the physical chaos of a tumbling piece of plastic requires serious computational logic. How does a computer code ensure that a "1" has the exact same mathematical probability as a "6"?

Here is a look at the fascinating computer science used behind the scenes to generate your digital roll:

### 1. The Core PRNG Engine
At the absolute core of the virtual roller is an algorithm known as a PRNG (Pseudo-Random Number Generator). Because computers are inherently logical, they cannot generate true "chaos." Instead, they use a massive, constantly shifting variable (like the exact current millisecond on the computer's internal clock) as a "seed" to start a complex mathematical equation.

### 2. The Modulo 6 Operation
Once the PRNG generates a massive, chaotic internal number (e.g., 8,493,201), the algorithm applies a strict mathematical boundary to simulate a six-sided die. It uses an operation to divide that massive number by 6 and calculates the remainder. 
*   Because the remainder of any number divided by 6 will always be between 0 and 5, the computer simply adds a "+1" to the final result. 
*   This perfectly forces the final output to always land cleanly between 1 and 6.

### 3. Eliminating Physical Bias
Mass-produced plastic dice are often horribly flawed. They can contain microscopic air bubbles inside the plastic, making one side slightly heavier than the other. Over hundreds of rolls, a heavy-sided die will absolutely favor specific numbers. The digital PRNG has zero mass, zero friction, and zero gravity. It is the purest, most mathematically perfect dice roll you can possibly achieve.
