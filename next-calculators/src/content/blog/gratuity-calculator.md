## Formula and Calculation Method

The calculator utilizes precise mathematical formulas depending on whether the employer is covered under the statutory act. Understanding these formulas highlights why the automated tool is so convenient.

**1. For Employees COVERED under the Payment of Gratuity Act:**
The law assumes a working month consists of 26 days. You are paid 15 days of salary for every completed year of service.

**Formula: Gratuity = (15 × Last Drawn Salary × Tenure) / 26**

*Tenure Rounding Rule:* If you have worked for more than 6 months in your final year, it is rounded up to the next full year. (e.g., 5 years and 7 months = 6 years).

**2. For Employees NOT COVERED under the Payment of Gratuity Act:**
The law assumes a standard 30-day working month. You are paid 15 days of salary for every completed year of service, but rounding is not permitted.

**Formula: Gratuity = (15 × Last Drawn Salary × Tenure) / 30**

*Tenure Rounding Rule:* No rounding is allowed. Only strictly completed years are counted. (e.g., 5 years and 11 months = exactly 5 years).

## Common Mistakes Users Make

When expecting a large final settlement, employees frequently set themselves up for disappointment by making basic calculation errors. Avoid these frequent pitfalls:

1.  **Using Gross Salary (CTC) Instead of Basic:** This is the most common and devastating mistake. If your total CTC is ₹1 Lakh per month, but your Basic Salary is only ₹40,000, your gratuity is calculated strictly on the ₹40,000. Never input your gross "take-home" pay into the calculator.
2.  **Misunderstanding the 5-Year Rule:** You must complete exactly 4 years and 240 days of continuous service (legally interpreted as 5 years) to be eligible for gratuity. If you resign at 4 years and 11 months, depending on specific court interpretations and company policy, you risk getting absolutely nothing.
3.  **Assuming the "Divide by 30" Formula:** Many employees manually calculate gratuity by dividing their salary by 30 days. If you are covered by the Act, the law strictly states a month is 26 working days. Dividing by 30 artificially shrinks your daily wage and lowers your final payout. Always use the calculator to apply the correct denominator.
4.  **Forgetting Notice Period Restrictions:** Gratuity is a statutory right and generally cannot be withheld just because you didn't serve a full notice period (though the company can deduct notice pay from your other settlements). However, it *can* be legally forfeited if you are terminated for violent conduct or intentional damage to company property.

## Frequently Asked Questions (FAQs)

**1. Is it mandatory to complete exactly 5 years to get Gratuity?**
Generally, yes. Section 4 of the Payment of Gratuity Act states continuous service of 5 years is mandatory. However, in cases of death or disablement due to an accident/disease, the 5-year requirement is completely waived, and gratuity is paid immediately.

**2. Can my employer refuse to pay gratuity?**
If you have completed 5 years of continuous service, your employer cannot legally refuse to pay. The only exception is if you were terminated for "moral turpitude" (serious crimes) or causing intentional damage to company property, in which case the gratuity can be partially or wholly forfeited.

**3. Does my maternity leave affect my continuous service for Gratuity?**
No. Approved maternity leave (up to 26 weeks), sick leave, and casual leave are all counted as days worked and do not break your "continuous service" record for the 5-year calculation.

**4. Is there a maximum limit on Gratuity?**
Yes. As per current Indian laws, the maximum amount of gratuity that is completely exempt from income tax is ₹20 Lakhs. An employer can choose to pay you more than this if they wish, but any amount above ₹20 Lakhs will be fully taxable as "Income from Salary."

**5. How is "Last Drawn Salary" defined?**
It is strictly defined as your Basic Salary plus your Dearness Allowance (DA). It expressly excludes any performance bonuses, House Rent Allowance (HRA), overtime pay, or travel reimbursements.

**6. What if my company is NOT covered under the Gratuity Act?**
If your company is not covered, they are not legally obligated to pay gratuity. However, many still do as an employee benefit. If they do, the formula changes: it uses a 30-day month (divide by 30) instead of a 26-day month, and you do not get to round up your tenure months.

**7. When must the employer pay the Gratuity amount?**
The employer is legally mandated to pay the gratuity amount within 30 days from the date it becomes payable (usually your last working day). If they delay payment beyond 30 days without a valid reason, they are liable to pay simple interest on the amount.

**8. Can I get Gratuity if I am a contract worker?**
If you are directly hired as a fixed-term contract worker by the company and complete 5 continuous years, you are generally eligible. However, if you are an independent contractor, freelancer, or hired through a third-party agency, the principal employer does not owe you gratuity.
